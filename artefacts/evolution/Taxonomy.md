## 3\. COMPUTED PILLAR SUMMARY

Not a separate semantic layer. A summary interface derived from the intra-pillar model output (Section 4.1.1 and 4.2.1) plus Primitives (Section 1.4), presenting each pillar's energy posture to the inter-pillar system.

### 3.1 Computation Rules

**Directionality** — computed from edges 1-3 (Stem ↔ hidden stem relationships): Each hidden stem relationship contributes a directional component: production from stem \= outward flow, production toward stem \= inward flow, control over stem \= downward pressure, stem controlling hidden stem \= dominance downward. Multiple hidden stems may contribute competing directions. Net directionality is the vector sum, weighted by hidden stem hierarchy (principal \> secondary \> residual).

**Retention** — computed from edges 1-6 (all internal relationships): Net of all inward and outward flows. All relationships feed stem and nothing drains \= maximum retention. All relationships drain stem and nothing feeds \= zero retention. Mixed \= partial. Weighted by hierarchy.

**Internal Coherence** — computed from edges 1-6: All relationships productive or same-element \= high coherence. Control relationships between hidden stems, or contradictory edges to stem \= low coherence. Determines signal clarity to inter-pillar system.

**Transformative Capacity** — computed from edges 4-6 (hidden stem ecology): Hidden stems forming a production chain \= transformative capacity exists. Chain length (2 or 3 elements) and direction determine conversion potential. No chain \= zero capacity.

**Self-Sufficiency** — computed from edges 1-3: Any hidden stem's element producing the stem's element \= resource present within pillar. Hierarchy level determines strength.

**Current Velocity** — computed from edges 4-6 \+ edge 7 \+ Branch Model 2.1: Active chain \+ stem participation \+ high vitality \+ no gating \= explosive. Single self-referencing element \+ high vitality \+ no chain \= still/deep. Gated chain \= slow-releasing until triggered. The kinetic state of the pillar's energy.

**Temperature** — computed from Section 1.4: Aggregate temperature of all elements present (stem \+ hidden stems), weighted by hierarchy. Position on hot ← → cold spectrum.

**Saturation** — computed from Section 1.4: Aggregate moisture of all elements present (stem \+ hidden stems), weighted by hierarchy. Position on wet ← → dry spectrum.

### 3.2 Usage

These summaries serve as the pillar's presentation to the inter-pillar system. When inter-pillar flow dynamics process interactions, each pillar's energy posture is read from this summary. Contains no new information beyond intra-pillar model and primitives. A convenience layer for efficient processing.

## 4\. NATAL FLOW

### 4.1 Composition Flow

The intrinsic energy currents within and between nodes. No Day Master reference needed. "What is here and how does it move."

#### 4.1.1 Intra-Pillar Composition Edges

For each pillar (natal or temporal):

**Stem ↔ Hidden Stem relationships:**

1. Stem ↔ Branch principal hidden stem: element relationship (production / control / drain / domination / same) \+ polarity relationship (same / opposite)  
2. Stem ↔ Branch secondary hidden stem (if exists): element relationship \+ polarity relationship  
3. Stem ↔ Branch residual hidden stem (if exists): element relationship \+ polarity relationship

**Internal Branch ecology (hidden stem ↔ hidden stem):**

4. Principal hidden stem ↔ secondary hidden stem (if exists): element relationship \+ polarity  
5. Principal hidden stem ↔ residual hidden stem (if exists): element relationship \+ polarity  
6. Secondary hidden stem ↔ residual hidden stem (if both exist): element relationship \+ polarity

**Vitality condition:**

7. Stem Life Stage on its own Branch (fixed for each of the 60 Stem-Branch combinations — from Section 1.8)

#### 4.1.2 Inter-Pillar Composition Flow

##### Relationship Dimensions

Between any two pillars, every entity (Stem, principal hidden stem, secondary hidden stem, residual hidden stem) in Pillar A relates to every entity in Pillar B along:

**Elemental relationship:** Production, control, drain, domination, sameness — the five-cycle relationship between the two entities' elements. Determines direction of flow.

**Polarity relationship:** Same polarity intensifies. Opposite polarity harmonizes.

**Vitality differential:** Life Stage of each entity on its own Branch determines push/receive capacity. Peak \= maximum throughput. Death \= sink with no output. Tomb \= gated. The differential determines actual edge throughput.

**Hierarchy differential:** Stem \> principal \> secondary \> residual. Creates weight gradient across every inter-pillar edge.

**Positional relationship:** Pillar domain pairing (from Section 1.7) colors every edge with life-domain significance.

**Proximity:** Adjacent pillars (Year-Month, Month-Day, Day-Hour) carry stronger inter-pillar edges than non-adjacent pairs.

**Climate interaction:** Temperature and saturation differential between pillars (from Section 3.1) affects energy behavior as it flows between them.

##### Propagation Principle

All inter-pillar relationships are **simultaneously determined**. No edge can be resolved independently.

The state of each node is the result of:

- Its intrinsic condition (intra-pillar composition flow)  
- The total flow entering it from all inter-pillar edges  
- The total flow leaving it through all inter-pillar edges

Each flow is determined by:

- Elemental relationship between source and destination  
- Vitality of source (push capacity)  
- Vitality of destination (receive capacity and retention)  
- Current state of source, which depends on all other flows entering and leaving it  
- Current state of destination, which depends on all other flows entering and leaving it

Circular by design. Resolves through **simultaneous constraint satisfaction**. The natal chart IS this equilibrium. Propagation is recursive and self-referential at every point. Attenuates by weight (hierarchy, proximity, vitality differential) but reaches everywhere.

##### Emergent Pattern Vocabulary

Shapes the topology naturally produces when the full system is instantiated:

**Chain:** Directional flow through multiple nodes. Character depends on each node's vitality, retention, and whether chain drains or accumulates. A chain through a Death-stage node is a conduit — everything passes through, nothing held.

**Loop:** Chain returning to origin. Closed loop circulates, may amplify or dampen per cycle. Unclosed loop drains without return.

**Pulse:** Oscillation within natal topology. Growth, combustion, output, depletion, growth again. Emerges when a node is simultaneously fed and consumed.

**Cascade:** Chain where each node amplifies the signal. Function × domain × vitality × polarity stack multiplicatively.

**Absence:** Element structurally missing from natal topology. Flows that would pass through it have nowhere to go. Moderation it would provide doesn't exist. Shapes every flow by non-existence.

**Bottleneck:** Node through which critical flow must pass, but whose capacity limits throughput. System capacity may be determined by narrowest bottleneck.

**Governor:** Element or node moderating a cascade or loop. Control-cycle relationships serve as governors. When absent, system runs without limit.

**Return path:** Flow replenishing the source of a drain chain. Determines sustainability. May be direct or indirect through multiple nodes.

**Resonance:** Nodes in similar states across different pillars creating sympathetic amplification. Structural similarity producing coherent system response.

**Contradiction:** Node where Composition Flow and Potential Flow push in opposite directions. Meaning is tension itself.

### 4.3 Topology-Modifying Interactions

Events that, when triggered, alter the flow topology itself — changing edge behavior, node states, entity identities, or flow patterns beyond what the baseline propagation principle produces.

- #### 4.3.1 Stem Combinations

- ##### Trigger Condition

- Activates whenever two Stems that form a combination pair (from Section 1.9) appear across pillar positions in the chart. This includes:

- Two natal Stems across different natal pillars  
- A natal Stem meeting a temporal Stem (arriving via Drift or Oscillation)  
- Two temporal Stems across different active temporal pillars

- ##### Combination States

- The combination operates on a spectrum of three states, determined by environmental conditions:

- **State 1 — Full Transformation**

- Both Stems abandon their original elemental identity and become the target element. This is a fundamental rewrite of the topology:

- *Composition Flow modifications:*

- Both Stems' element changes to the target element. Every intra-pillar edge (1-3) recalculates for both affected pillars because the Stem is now a different element relating to the same hidden stems.  
- The Computed Pillar Summary (Section 3\) recalculates for both pillars — directionality, retention, coherence, temperature, saturation all shift because the Stem has changed.  
- All inter-pillar edges from both Stems recalculate — every entity in every other pillar now relates to a different element at those positions.

- *Potential Flow modifications:*

- If the Day Master is one of the combining Stems: the Day Master's elemental identity changes. The ENTIRE Potential Flow plane recalculates — every Ten God assignment in the chart shifts because the center has changed. All domain resonance edges (12-15) shift. The Useful God assessment (16) potentially changes.  
- If the Day Master is not involved: both combining Stems receive new Ten God identities relative to the unchanged Day Master. Their functional roles in the chart change.

- *Conditions required:*

- Month Branch must empower the target element (seasonal support — the target element must be Prosperous or at minimum supported in the current season)  
- Surrounding chart must not strongly oppose the target element (no overwhelming presence of the element that controls the target)  
- No clash or separation force actively disrupting the bond between the two Stems

- **State 2 — Partial Combination (attraction without transformation)**

- Both Stems retain original elemental identity but operate at reduced capacity. The topology is modified not by rewriting elements but by diminishing throughput:

- *Composition Flow modifications:*

- All edges from both Stems (intra-pillar edges 1-3 and all inter-pillar edges) operate at reduced capacity. Energy that would normally flow through these edges is partially redirected toward the bond between the two Stems.  
- The two pillar positions become linked — a new inter-pillar edge exists between them that represents the attraction itself, drawing energy from both Stems toward the bond rather than toward the system.

- *Potential Flow modifications:*

- Both Stems' Ten God functions are diluted. A Seven Killings that is partially combined is less aggressive. A Resource that is partially combined is less nourishing. The Ten God role still exists but operates at reduced power.  
- The pillar domains of both positions become entangled per Section 1.7 bond meanings.  
- If the Day Master is involved: the Day Master operates at reduced capacity — partially pulled toward an identity it cannot fully become. The center of the Potential Field is weakened not by external drain but by internal division.

- *Conditions:* This is the default state when the combination pair appears but transformation conditions are not met.

- **State 3 — Disrupted Combination**

- The structural attraction exists but an active force prevents bonding. Minimal topology modification:

- The attraction exists as a latent structural property — readiness that could activate if the disrupting force is removed.  
- Both Stems operate at near-full capacity in their original functions.  
- If the disrupting force is temporal (a clashing Branch that will eventually depart), the combination may shift from State 3 to State 2 or State 1 when the disruption ends.

- *Conditions:* A third Stem or Branch is actively clashing with one of the combining Stems, or a competing combination has captured one of the Stems (a single Stem cannot participate in two combinations simultaneously).

- ##### Neutralization Principle

- When a Stem is combined away (State 1 or State 2), its original Ten God function is redirected. This is indiscriminate:

- A hostile Ten God (Seven Killings, Hurting Officer) combined away has its aggression redirected into the bond. The attack on the Day Master is reduced proportionally to the combination's strength.  
- A beneficial Ten God (Resource, Eating God) combined away has its support redirected into the bond. The nourishment or output is reduced proportionally.

- The combination does not distinguish between helpful and harmful. It redirects whatever it captures.

- ##### Structural Properties

- Each pair arises from the controlling cycle: the yang Stem controls the yin Stem's element. The combination transforms the controlling relationship into merger — the controller dissolves into the controlled rather than dominating it.  
- Proximity affects combination strength: adjacent pillars (Year-Month, Day-Hour) form stronger bonds than distant pairs (Year-Hour). The Month-Day combination is among the strongest because the Month pillar carries maximum seasonal authority.  
- A Stem can only participate in one active combination at a time. If multiple potential combination partners exist, the nearest (by pillar proximity) takes priority, with the Day Master's involvement overriding proximity.

#### 4.3.2 Branch Interactions

Branch interactions are topology-modifying events triggered when specific Branch pairs or groups (from Section 2.2 Fixed Interaction Valence) appear across pillar positions. They operate between the grounds — the environments beneath the Stems — and modify the flow topology at the Branch level, with effects propagating upward to the Stems above.

##### 4.3.2.1 Six Harmonies (六合)

**Trigger Condition:** Activates whenever two Branches that form a harmony pair (from Section 2.2) appear across pillar positions. Includes natal-to-natal, natal-to-temporal, and temporal-to-temporal pairings.

**Harmony States:**

Parallel to Stem Combinations (Section 4.3.1), harmonies operate on a spectrum of three states:

**State 1 — Full Transformation**

Both Branches abandon their original elemental identities and merge into the target element. This is a fundamental rewrite of both nodes' ground:

*Composition Flow modifications:*

- All hidden stems in both Branches are overwritten by the target element. The internal Branch ecology (edges 4-6) is dissolved — there are no longer distinct hidden stems in their original elemental identities. Both Branches now present as the target element.  
- Stem ↔ hidden stem edges (1-3) recalculate for both pillars because the ground beneath each Stem has changed elemental identity.  
- Life Stage (edge 7\) recalculates — each Stem now sits on a Branch whose element has changed, placing the Stem at a different Life Stage.  
- Computed Pillar Summary (Section 3\) recalculates for both pillars — directionality, retention, coherence, velocity, temperature, saturation all shift.  
- All inter-pillar edges from both Branches' entities recalculate.  
- Storage gating (Section 2.1) is dissolved if either Branch was a Storage Branch — the sealed element no longer exists in its original form.

*Potential Flow modifications:*

- All hidden stem Ten God identities (edges 9-11) recalculate relative to Day Master because the hidden stems' elements have changed.  
- Domain resonance (edges 12-15) recalculates.  
- The two pillar domains become linked per Section 1.7 bond meanings.

*Conditions required:*

- Month Branch must empower the target element (seasonal support)  
- Surrounding chart must not strongly oppose the target element  
- No clash actively disrupting either Branch in the pair  
- No competing harmony or combination claiming either Branch

**State 2 — Partial Harmony (attraction without transformation)**

Both Branches retain original identities but are magnetically pulled toward each other. Edges from both Branches operate at reduced capacity:

*Composition Flow modifications:*

- Hidden stem edges from both Branches operate at reduced throughput — energy is partially redirected toward the bond rather than flowing through normal channels.  
- A new inter-pillar edge exists between the two Branches representing the attraction itself, drawing energy from both grounds toward potential merger.  
- The two pillar domains are linked per Section 1.7 bond meanings.  
- Neither Branch's internal ecology is dissolved — hidden stems retain original identities but are partially engaged in the pull toward transformation.

*Potential Flow modifications:*

- Hidden stem Ten God functions are diluted proportionally to the harmony's pull strength.  
- Domain linkage colors both positions with the bond meaning.

*Conditions:* Default state when harmony pair appears but transformation conditions are not met.

**State 3 — Disrupted Harmony**

Attraction exists structurally but an active force prevents bonding. Minimal topology modification — latent readiness. Both Branches operate at near-full capacity. May shift to State 2 or 1 if disrupting force departs.

##### 4.3.2.2 Six Clashes (六冲)

**Trigger Condition:** Activates whenever two Branches that form a clash pair (from Section 2.2) appear across pillar positions. Includes natal-to-natal, natal-to-temporal, and temporal-to-temporal pairings.

**Topology Modifications:**

Unlike harmonies and combinations, clashes do not operate on a transformation spectrum. They are disruptive events. Their intensity varies (bounded by the strength factors feeding into Section 4.3.3) but their character is always disruption.

*Composition Flow modifications:*

- **Hidden stem scattering:** The hidden stems inside both clashing Branches are destabilized — shaken loose from their normal positions. Edges from hidden stems (1-3 and 4-6) become volatile. The internal Branch ecology is disrupted rather than dissolved — the elements are still present but their orderly relationships are broken up. Scattered hidden stems become individually available to the inter-pillar system in ways they weren't before — elements that were part of an internal chain now float independently.

- **Specific releases by clash pair:**

  - 子午 Zi-Wu (Water vs Fire): Pure Water and peak Fire collide — steam. Both elements disrupted. No secondary hidden stems released from Zi (pure Branch). Ding Fire and Ji Earth released from Wu.  
  - 丑未 Chou-Wei (Earth vs Earth — two vaults): Stored Metal (Xin) released from Chou. Stored Wood (Yi) released from Wei. Both storage gates forced open. Both vaults' contents become active in the chart.  
  - 寅申 Yin-Shen (Wood vs Metal): Hidden Fire (Bing) and Earth (Wu) released from Yin. Hidden Water (Ren) and Earth (Wu) released from Shen. Multi-element upheaval — secondary and residual energies from both Branches scatter.  
  - 卯酉 Mao-You (Wood vs Metal): Pure Yi Wood and pure Xin Metal collide. No secondary stems to release. Clean, precise, surgical disruption. Both elements diminished.  
  - 辰戌 Chen-Xu (Earth vs Earth — two vaults): Stored Water (Gui) released from Chen. Stored Fire (Ding) released from Xu. Both storage gates forced open. Seismic — two massive Earth structures cracking simultaneously.  
  - 巳亥 Si-Hai (Fire vs Water): Hidden Metal (Geng) and Earth (Wu) released from Si. Hidden Wood (Jia) released from Hai. Both alchemical processes interrupted — what was being forged and what was being gestated are released prematurely.

- **Stem destabilization:** The Stems sitting above clashing Branches lose stable ground. Their intra-pillar edges (1-3) become unreliable — the Stem's relationship to its Branch is disrupted because the Branch itself is in upheaval. The Stem doesn't change element, but its functional capacity is diminished by the instability beneath it.

- **Life Stage disruption:** The Stem's Life Stage (edge 7\) on its own Branch becomes unreliable — the vitality condition is destabilized. A Stem at Peak on a clashed Branch doesn't operate at full Peak capacity.

- **Harmony disruption:** If either clashing Branch was participating in an active harmony (State 1 or 2\) with a third Branch, the clash breaks that harmony. The harmony reverts to State 3 (disrupted) or dissolves entirely. This is the mechanism by which Harms operate (Section 4.3.2.6).

*Potential Flow modifications:*

- Hidden stem Ten God functions become volatile — the scattered elements are available but unpredictable in how they serve the chart.  
- Domain resonance destabilizes — the pillar positions involved carry the clash/disruption meaning from Section 1.7.  
- Released elements may introduce Ten God energies that were previously locked (especially from storage Branch clashes) — a stored Seven Killings that was gated suddenly becomes active.

**Seasonal Modifier:** The seasonal element (determined by Month Branch sovereignty, Section 6.1) tilts the clash. In the season of one element in the clash pair, that element's Branch dominates — it disrupts the opposing Branch more than it is disrupted. In neutral seasons, both are equally affected. This modulates the asymmetry of the disruption but does not prevent it.

##### 4.3.2.3 Three Harmony Frames (三合)

**Trigger Condition:** Activates when two or three Branches from the same Frame (from Section 2.2) appear across pillar positions. Two Branches \= half-frame. Three Branches \= full frame.

**Half-Frame (two of three present):**

*Topology modifications:*

- The two present Branches are linked — their shared elemental identity is partially active. The target element begins to assert itself across both pillar positions.  
- The link is weaker than a full frame — it creates a structural readiness, a latent coalition waiting for its third member.  
- When the missing Branch arrives via temporal flow (Luck Pillar, annual pillar, etc.), the half-frame completes and the full frame activates.  
- The specific character of each half-frame depends on which member is missing:  
  - Birth \+ Peak without Storage: powerful current with no reservoir — energy generated and expressed but not preserved.  
  - Peak \+ Storage without Birth: depth and preservation without generative source — reserves exist but aren't being replenished.  
  - Birth \+ Storage without Peak: generation and preservation without full expression — the spring and the vault exist but the river between them hasn't formed.

**Full Frame (all three present):**

*Composition Flow modifications:*

- The three Branches create a dominant elemental force of the target element. This force is stronger than any individual Branch and can overpower opposing elements in the chart.  
- All three Branches' energies are unified under the target element. Hidden stems that don't belong to the target element are subdued — they still exist but their edges operate at reduced capacity, overwhelmed by the dominant elemental coalition.  
- The three pillar domains involved become unified under the frame's elemental sovereignty. Whatever those domains individually meant is subordinated to the coalition's agenda.  
- Inter-pillar edges from all three positions are colored by the dominant element — they project the target element's energy outward into the rest of the chart.

*Potential Flow modifications:*

- The dominant element shifts the chart's elemental balance, which affects every Ten God calculation. If the frame's element is the Day Master's Resource, the Day Master is massively supported. If it's the Day Master's Output, the Day Master is massively drained.  
- The Useful God assessment (edge 16\) may shift — the frame may provide what was needed or may overwhelm what was balanced.  
- Three pillar domains unified means three life areas operating as one — per Section 1.7, the specific combination of Year/Month/Day/Hour positions determines which domains merge.

**Relationship to individual Branch interactions:** Branches participating in a full frame may simultaneously hold other interaction valences (a Branch in the Fire frame may also be a clash partner with a Branch outside the frame). The frame's dominance may suppress or amplify these individual interactions depending on their alignment with the frame's element — this feeds into the simulation (Section 4.3.3).

##### 4.3.2.4 Directional Combinations (三会 / 方合)

**Trigger Condition:** Activates when all three consecutive seasonal Branches from the same Directional group (from Section 2.2) appear across pillar positions. Unlike Three Harmony Frames, there is no half-combination — all three must be present for activation. (When two of three are present, this creates structural readiness but no active topology modification beyond whatever individual Branch interactions those two Branches independently trigger.)

**Topology Modifications:**

The most powerful combination in the system. Produces an overwhelming tidal wave of a single element.

*Composition Flow modifications:*

- All three Branches' energies merge into a single elemental force of the directional element. This is more powerful than a full Three Harmony Frame because it represents a complete, uninterrupted season rather than a strategic alliance of three distant points.  
- Individual hidden stem identities within the three Branches are effectively overwritten — the directional element's sovereignty is near-absolute across all three positions.  
- Stems sitting above the three Branches experience a fundamentally altered ground — regardless of their individual Stem-on-Branch chemistry, the ground beneath them is now a unified elemental field.  
- The chart's aggregate climate (temperature and saturation) shifts dramatically toward the directional element's climate signature.  
- Opposing elements are suppressed chart-wide — not just within the three participating pillars but across the entire system, because the elemental balance has been massively tilted.

*Potential Flow modifications:*

- Equivalent to Three Harmony Frame effects but more extreme. The dominant element's Ten God relationship to the Day Master becomes the dominant force in the chart.  
- If the directional element controls the Day Master, the pressure is immense. If it produces the Day Master, the support is immense. If it IS the Day Master's element, the self is overwhelmed by its own nature — same-element saturation.

**Seasonal note:** A Directional Combination IS a complete season. If it occupies or includes the Month Branch position, its element holds undisputed seasonal sovereignty (Section 6.1).

##### 4.3.2.5 Three Punishments (三刑)

**Trigger Condition:** Activates when two or three Branches from the same punishment group (from Section 2.2) appear across pillar positions. Two Branches \= half-punishment. Three Branches \= full punishment. Self-punishment activates when the same Branch appears in two different pillar positions.

**Topology Modifications:**

Punishments differ fundamentally from clashes. A clash is a momentary collision — violent, disruptive, potentially liberating. A punishment is a **chronic friction pattern** — grinding, repetitive, wearing. The topology is not broken open but abraded.

*Composition Flow modifications:*

- **Ongoing edge stress:** Unlike clashes which scatter hidden stems in a single disruptive event, punishments create persistent stress on the edges between participating Branches. The edges don't break — they grind. The flow through them is chronically distorted, never settling into clean patterns.

- **By punishment type:**

  - 寅巳申 Yin-Si-Shen (Unchecked Power): The three Branches form a closed loop of mutual control — Metal (Shen) controls Wood (Yin), Wood feeds Fire (Si), Fire controls Metal (Shen). Each node is simultaneously attacker and attacked. The topology modification is a **grinding cycle** where energy circulates through the three positions without resolution — each gain at one node is immediately attacked by another. No node can accumulate. Edges between the three are permanently stressed, high throughput but zero retention.

  - 丑未戌 Chou-Wei-Xu (Persistent Bullying): Three Earth Storage Branches whose stored contents attack each other — stored Metal (Chou) attacks stored Wood (Wei), stored Fire (Xu) attacks stored Metal (Chou), stored Wood (Wei) feeds stored Fire (Xu). The gating mechanism (Section 2.1) is partially overridden — the stored elements become active enough to attack each other but not fully released as in a clash. The topology modification is **chronic internal warfare** within the storage system — reserves deplete each other. The erosion is slow, domestic, and ongoing.

  - 子卯 Zi-Mao (Uncivilized): Water (Zi) produces Wood (Mao) — a relationship that should be nurturing. The punishment occurs because the production crosses boundaries — the feeding becomes excessive, unregulated, suffocating. The topology modification is a **production edge without governor** — the flow from Zi to Mao has no natural limit. The Water pours into the Wood without restraint. The nourishment becomes control. The edge exists in perpetual overflow.

  - Self-Punishment 子子, 午午, 酉酉, 亥亥: The same Branch in two pillar positions. The topology modification is **resonance amplification to the point of self-damage**. The two identical nodes create sympathetic vibration at the same frequency — each reinforces the other's characteristics to excess. Water becomes too deep (depression, paralysis). Fire becomes too hot (mania, burnout). Metal becomes too precise (perfectionism, rigidity). Water-in-gestation becomes too potential (permanent latency, nothing manifests). The two pillar domains involved (per Section 1.7) both saturate with the same elemental excess.

*Potential Flow modifications:*

- The Ten God functions of entities in punished Branches are chronically stressed rather than acutely disrupted. They still operate but under constant friction — like a machine running with sand in the gears.  
- Domain linkage carries the punishment character: the life areas involved are locked in repetitive, unresolvable friction. The pattern replays.

**Half-Punishment (two of three in triangles):** Each pair within the triangle carries its own specific friction quality. The topology modification is a subset of the full punishment — the grinding pattern is present but with only two nodes involved, the closed-loop quality is incomplete. The friction is directional (A grinding against B) rather than circular (A → B → C → A).

##### 4.3.2.6 Six Harms (六害)

**Trigger Condition:** Activates whenever two Branches that form a harm pair (from Section 2.2) appear across pillar positions.

**Topology Modifications:**

Harms are the most indirect interaction type. They do not directly modify the edge between the two participating Branches. Instead, they operate through **interference with a third-party relationship**.

*Mechanism:* Every harm pair exists because one Branch disrupts a harmony that the other Branch was participating in. The topology modification is:

1. Branch A is in a harmony pair with Branch C (from Section 2.2 harmony partners).  
2. Branch B is in a clash pair with Branch C (from Section 2.2 clash partners).  
3. When Branch A and Branch B are both present, Branch B's clash valence toward C creates a structural threat to A's harmony with C — even if C is not present in the chart.  
4. If C IS present: B actively clashes C, which disrupts the A-C harmony. The harm is realized through the clash.  
5. If C is NOT present: B's disruptive affinity toward C still colors its relationship with A. The harm manifests as an indirect corrosion — A's capacity to form its harmony is undermined by B's presence. The harmony valence on A is weakened. If C arrives temporally, the harmony forms more weakly than it would without B present.

*Composition Flow modifications:*

- The harmed Branch (A) has its harmony valence reduced. Its capacity to form or maintain its natural harmony is diminished.  
- The harming Branch (B) doesn't directly lose capacity — it operates normally. The damage is asymmetric. B damages A's alliance without damaging itself.  
- The edge between A and B is not a clash edge — it's a **corrosion edge**. It doesn't scatter or break. It slowly degrades the positive relational capacity of the harmed Branch.

*Potential Flow modifications:*

- The pillar domains involved carry a sabotage quality — one life area undermining another's positive alliances. The specific domain pairing (from Section 1.7) determines where the interference is felt.  
- The harmed Branch's Ten God functions may lose support they would have received from a healthy harmony — the indirect damage ripples through the Potential plane as reduced support or reduced transformation potential.

##### 4.3.2.7 Branch Interaction Effects on Stems Above

All Branch interactions propagate upward to affect the Stems sitting on the participating Branches. This is a universal principle across all six interaction types:

- **Harmonies (State 1 — full transformation):** The Stem now sits on transformed ground. Its intra-pillar edges (1-3) recalculate entirely. Its Life Stage (edge 7\) changes. Its Computed Pillar Summary shifts.

- **Harmonies (State 2 — partial):** The Stem sits on ground that is partially pulled away. Its intra-pillar edges operate at reduced reliability — the ground is not fully what it was.

- **Clashes:** The Stem sits on destabilized ground. Its intra-pillar edges become unreliable. Its vitality condition (edge 7\) is diminished. The Stem's capacity to participate in inter-pillar flows is reduced because its foundation is in upheaval.

- **Three Harmony Frames and Directional Combinations:** The Stem sits on ground that has been absorbed into a dominant elemental force. Its individual Stem-on-Branch chemistry is subordinated to the coalition's energy.

- **Punishments:** The Stem sits on chronically stressed ground. Its intra-pillar edges function but under persistent friction. The Stem's capacity is not acutely reduced but chronically impaired.

- **Harms:** The Stem sitting on the harmed Branch experiences reduced relational capacity — its ground's positive alliance potential is corroded. The Stem sitting on the harming Branch is largely unaffected.

In all cases, the upward propagation follows the same rule: **Branch state changes → intra-pillar edges recalculate → Computed Pillar Summary recalculates → inter-pillar edges from that pillar recalculate → system-wide propagation per Section 4.1.2 propagation principle.**

#### 4.3.3 Interaction Priority and Weight

When multiple topology-modifying interactions are simultaneously active, the system does not resolve through fixed priority rules. Instead, each interaction's strength is determined by a set of **range-bounded factors** derived from structural properties already in the model, and the resolution is found through **simulation** that produces a probability landscape of possible system states.

##### Strength Factors (ranges, not fixed values)

Each topology-modifying interaction's strength is bounded by:

- **Vitality of participating entities:** Life Stage (edge 7\) of all Stems and hidden stems involved. Peak \= high strength ceiling. Death \= low ceiling.  
- **Hierarchy level of participating entities:** Stem-level interactions carry more weight than hidden-stem-level.  
- **Proximity of pillars involved:** Adjacent pillars form stronger interactions than distant pairs.  
- **Seasonal support:** Month Branch sovereignty (Section 6.1) empowering or opposing the interaction's element. Sovereign support \= high ceiling. Sovereign opposition \= low ceiling.  
- **Chart-wide elemental balance:** Overall presence of relevant elements across all pillars. Strong presence \= reinforcement. Absence \= no support.  
- **Internal coherence of participating pillars:** From Computed Summary (Section 3). High coherence \= clean signal supporting the interaction. Low coherence \= noisy signal.  
- **Reinforcement or opposition from other active topology modifiers:** Whether other interactions in the system support or resist this one.

Each factor contributes a **range of influence** — wide when the factor is ambiguous (moderate vitality, mixed seasonal support), narrow when extreme (Peak vitality, sovereign seasonal power).

##### Simulation Principle

When multiple topology modifiers are active simultaneously, the system does not resolve to a single state through priority rules. Instead:

- Each modifier's strength is computed as a range from its factors.  
- The interaction space of all simultaneously active modifiers is explored.  
- The result is a **probability landscape** of possible topology states, each weighted by internal consistency across all active forces operating within their ranges.  
- Some regions dominate (high probability — system "mostly" resolves this way). Some are possible but unlikely. Some are genuinely equiprobable — system exists in real superposition.  
- The landscape shifts whenever the temporal environment changes — new Drift or Oscillation pillars reconfigure the ranges, reshaping the probability landscape.

##### Reading the Landscape

A chart reading is not a single deterministic output. It is a reading of the probability landscape — what states are most likely, what conditions tip the balance, where genuine ambiguity lives, and how temporal flow will shift the landscape over the life trajectory.

## 6\. GOVERNANCE RULES

Structural laws that govern the entire system. These are not interaction types or flow dynamics — they are the foundational constraints that all other sections depend on.

### 6.1 Day Stem Sovereignty

The Day Stem IS the self. It is the center of the Potential Flow plane (Section 4.2). All Ten God identities in the system are computed relative to the Day Stem's element and polarity. All domain resonance assessments reference the Day Stem's perspective. The Useful God is defined as what the Day Stem's system most needs. If the Day Stem's elemental identity changes (via Stem Combination State 1, Section 4.3.1), the entire Potential Flow plane recalculates — every Ten God, every domain resonance, every Useful God assessment shifts because the center has moved.

The Day Branch is the Day Stem's intimate ground — the spouse palace, the body's ground, the felt experience of daily existence. It is the only Branch in the system that defines a life domain specifically through its structural relationship to the Day Stem rather than through its positional sequence (Year-Month-Hour are sequential; Day Branch is relational to the self).

### 6.2 Month Branch Sovereignty

Whatever Branch occupies the Month position determines which element holds sovereign power over the entire chart. The specific element empowered is derivable from the Branch's elemental composition. This sovereignty:

- Determines seasonal authority — which element is Prosperous, which is weakened  
- Serves as the primary condition for Stem Combination transformation success (Section 4.3.1)  
- Serves as the primary condition for Branch Harmony transformation success (Section 4.3.2.1)  
- Tilts clash outcomes via the seasonal modifier (Section 4.3.2.2)  
- Is the highest-weight factor in the interaction strength ranges (Section 4.3.3)

Applies to the natal Month Branch and to temporal Month Branches (which temporarily shift sovereignty).

- **Dead (死):** The element controlled by the Prosperous element. Dominated.

### 6.4 Useful God Determination

The Useful God (用神) is the element the chart most needs for balance. It creates a system-wide gradient across the Potential Flow plane (Section 4.2.2) — every node is assessed for alignment with or opposition to the Useful God's function.

#### 6.4.1 Classical Heuristic (Initialization Layer)

The classical method provides a rapid initial assessment. It operates through three steps:

**Step 1 — Day Master Strength Assessment**

The Day Master's strength is assessed across two dimensions:

*Support factors (strengthen the Day Master):*

- Seasonal vitality: Day Master's element Prosperous or Strong in the current season (Section 6.3)  
- Same-element presence: Companions and Rivals across all pillars, weighted by hierarchy level and vitality  
- Resource presence: Resource stars (element producing the Day Master) across all pillars, weighted by hierarchy and vitality  
- Root: Day Master's element appearing as hidden stem in any Branch — foothold in the terrain  
- Day Branch support: Day Branch containing Day Master's own element or resource element

*Drain factors (weaken the Day Master):*

- Seasonal hostility: Day Master's element Imprisoned or Dead in the current season  
- Output drain: Eating Gods and Hurting Officers across all pillars, weighted by hierarchy and vitality  
- Wealth drain: Wealth stars across all pillars — Day Master spends energy controlling them  
- Authority pressure: Officers and Seven Killings across all pillars — pressing down on Day Master  
- Lack of root: Day Master's element absent from all Branch hidden stems

*Assessment produces a spectrum:*

- **Very Strong (极旺):** Prosperous in season, multiple Companions/Resources, dominates chart  
- **Strong (旺):** Well-supported — more energy flowing in than out  
- **Balanced (中和):** Roughly equal support and drain — rare  
- **Weak (弱):** Under-supported — more energy flowing out than in  
- **Very Weak (极弱):** Day Master's element nearly absent, overwhelmed by opposing forces

**Step 2 — Useful God Selection**

The prescription follows from the diagnosis:

*Strong Day Master — needs drain and control:*

- Output (Eating God / Hurting Officer): channels excess into production — generally preferred  
- Wealth (Direct / Indirect): gives excess something to control — practical  
- Authority (Officer / Seven Killings): contains excess directly — powerful but harsh

*Weak Day Master — needs feeding and reinforcement:*

- Resource (Direct / Indirect): produces the Day Master's element — generally preferred  
- Companion (Equal / Rival): reinforces Day Master's presence — supportive but doesn't generate

The Useful God is whichever element the chart most lacks among the appropriate category.

**Step 3 — Extreme Case Detection**

When the Day Master is at the extremes of the spectrum, the standard logic may invert:

*Very Strong — potential Follow Strength (从旺):* If the Day Master's element is so dominant that drain/control cannot meaningfully reduce it, the chart may function better by supporting the dominant element rather than opposing it. The Useful God becomes what further feeds the Day Master.

*Very Weak — potential Follow the Leader (从格):* If the Day Master is so overwhelmed that support cannot meaningfully rescue it, the chart may function better by surrendering to the dominant element. ### A1.4 Energy Function Construction

The energy function E(state) measures total inconsistency across all factors:

E(state) \= Σ over all intra-pillar factors \+ Σ over all inter-pillar factors \+ Σ over all topology-modifier factors \+ cross-plane consistency penalty

Each factor contributes an inconsistency score. The score is zero when the constraint is perfectly satisfied, and increases with the degree of violation.

**Key structural properties of E:**

- **Recursive coupling:** Entity state variables appear in multiple factors simultaneously (an entity's element participates in intra-pillar chemistry, inter-pillar flows, topology modifier conditions, and Ten God calculations). Changing one entity's state ripples through all factors that reference it. This encodes the Indra's Net property — the energy function structurally represents the mutual determination of all nodes.

- **Discrete-continuous mixing:** The topology-mode switches are discrete (a harmony is either State 1, State 2, State 3, or Off). The interaction strengths are continuous within their ranges. The energy function must handle both — some factors constrain discrete choices, others constrain continuous values conditional on the discrete state.

- **Range-bounded strengths:** Interaction strength variables are not free — they are bounded by ranges derived from structural properties (Section 4.3.3 strength factors). The energy function must penalize strength values outside their structurally determined ranges.

- **Asymmetric factor weights:** Not all factors are equally important. Month pillar sovereignty (Section 6.1) carries more authority than Hour pillar edges. Stem-level interactions carry more significance than residual-hidden-stem interactions. The hierarchy of factor weights reflects the taxonomy's own hierarchy: seasonal authority \> pillar proximity \> entity hierarchy \> all else.

**The target distribution:**

P(state) ∝ exp(-E(state) / ω)

Where ω (temperature) controls landscape sharpness. High ω \= diffuse, many states roughly equiprobable, maximum superposition. Low ω \= sharp, dominant attractors clearly visible.

### A1.5 Inference Specification

**Method:** Tempered Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC).

**Particle population:** Each particle is one complete state configuration (all entity states \+ all structural descriptors \+ all topology-mode switches \+ all interaction strengths). The population of particles represents the probability landscape.

**Tempering schedule:** Bridge from high ω (diffuse — everything possible) to target ω (calibrated sharpness). At each temperature step:

1. **Reweight** particles by the ratio of successive target densities.  
2. **Rejuvenate** via blocked MCMC moves:  
   - **Discrete block:** Propose topology-mode switch changes (flip a harmony from State 2 to State 1, activate/deactivate a clash). Use generalized belief propagation as proposal engine for the discrete block.  
   - **Continuous block:** Propose interaction strength changes within their ranges, conditional on current discrete topology. Use standard MCMC (e.g., Hamiltonian Monte Carlo) within the continuous block.  
   - **Entity state block:** Propose element rewrites (for entities involved in active State 1 transformations) and propagate all downstream factor recalculations.

**Temporal node arrival/departure (Section 5.5):**

When a temporal pillar enters or exits:

1. Update the supergraph: activate or deactivate the temporal node's entity state variables, add or remove all edges connecting it to other nodes, activate or deactivate all topology-modifier switches involving the temporal node.  
2. **Reweight** existing particle population to the new posterior (the energy function now includes or excludes factors involving the temporal node).  
3. **Bridge** from current population to new equilibrium via additional tempering steps if the posterior has shifted significantly.  
4. This produces continuous landscape evolution — the probability landscape deforms smoothly as temporal nodes arrive and depart rather than being recomputed from scratch.

**Frequency stacking (Section 5.5, point 6):**

When multiple temporal frequencies align (same element at hour, day, month, year, Luck Pillar), the energy function has multiple reinforcing factors all favoring the same element. This naturally produces a sharp attractor basin around states where that element dominates — the multiplicative amplification emerges from the factor structure without needing explicit multiplication rules.

### A1.6 Post-Processing Specification

**Sample-then-relax:**

1. From the final particle population at target ω, take each particle and **relax** it to a nearby local energy minimum (fixed point). This polishes noisy samples into clean state configurations.

2. **Cluster** the relaxed particles into attractor basins — groups of particles that relaxed to the same or very similar fixed points.

3. **Report:**

   - Basin identities: what is the characteristic topology of each basin? Map each basin back to the emergent pattern vocabulary (Section 4.1.2) — identify the chains, loops, pulses, cascades, absences, bottlenecks, governors, return paths that characterize each attractor.  
   - Probability masses: what fraction of particles landed in each basin? This is the probability of each reading.  
   - Superposition characterization: how many basins have significant mass? One dominant basin \= clear reading. Multiple significant basins \= genuine ambiguity, the chart exists in superposition between these states.  
   - Temporal sensitivity: for each basin, which temporal conditions would shift particles toward or away from it? This maps to "when does the reading change" — identifying the Luck Pillars, years, months, days, hours that would reconfigure the landscape.
